# Opening Presentation (4–5 minutes)

## Strategy: Lead with your strongest analytical move

Pick the topic where you have the most depth and can defend all follow-up questions. Two options:

---

## Option A: The Source Criticism Argument (Recommended)

**Why:** This shows you are an independent, critical thinker — the hardest quality criterion to fake. It also explains and justifies your hybrid governance stance without just asserting it.

**Draft (4–5 min):**

> In this paper, I advise Martin Hasgard Olesen on how to approach the executive discussion about Coop App and Lobyco.
>
> My central argument is this: the available evidence cannot support a strong recommendation to either massively invest in, or cut, the app. The honest strategic response is hybrid governance — preserve the digital capabilities, but tie further investment to demonstrated business-model value.
>
> The reason for this stance starts with source criticism. The key claims about Coop App's value — 1.8 million users, 25% household reach, the claim that app users shop more often — all come from Lobyco, Playable, or Shortcut. These are commercially interested parties. They document the first level of value creation: adoption and engagement. But they cannot document what matters strategically: incremental store economics, margin contribution, or causal effect on customer behaviour.
>
> This creates a four-level evidence gap:
> Adoption → customer behaviour → store economics → profitability
>
> The public sources speak to level 1. Levels 3 and 4 are undocumented.
>
> Applying DVC and Digital Business Model together makes this concrete. DVC shows where digital value *could* exist — experiences, relationships, relevance, infrastructure. DBM asks the harder question: does that potential value get *captured* in a way that fits Coop's store-first business model? Given EBIT of -215 million DKK, Coop cannot invest in digital activity that doesn't translate to financial discipline.
>
> My five recommendations follow from this. The first — redefine app success from engagement metrics to business-model fit — is the governance change that makes all the others possible. Without it, Coop risks confusing a 1.8M-user reach figure with strategic competitive advantage.

---

## Option B: Theory Application — Why DVC + DBM

**Why:** Good if you want to demonstrate theory mastery upfront.

**Draft (4–5 min):**

> The central methodological choice in this paper is to use DVC Framework and Digital Business Model perspective together. I want to explain why this pairing is analytically necessary — and what it reveals about Coop's situation.
>
> DVC Framework gives us five analytical dimensions: experiences, relationships, relevance, digital infrastructure, and digital outputs. Applied to Coop App, it shows genuine potential: Scan & Betal improves the store experience, personalised offers strengthen the member relationship, and relevance can be differentiated across Coop's four chains.
>
> But DVC has a blind spot. It can make digital activity look inherently valuable. If the app has 880,000 monthly active users, DVC might suggest: "this creates experience and relationship value." That's not wrong — but it's incomplete.
>
> That's where Digital Business Model perspective comes in. Weill and Woerner force the question: does this digital value actually get *captured* in a way that fits the business model? Coop is a store-first omnichannel business. After closing Coop.dk in January 2025, its strategic identity is clear: the physical store is the business model. App value must be measured through store traffic, margin, and chain-specific relevance — not through engagement metrics.
>
> The combination of the two theories gives me a complete analytical frame: DVC maps the possibilities, DBM tests them against financial and strategic reality. And the result is my hybrid governance stance: Coop has genuine digital assets worth preserving, but further investment must be conditional on demonstrating that DVC-level value translates to DBM-level value capture.

---

## Tips for the oral

- Speak at a measured pace — 4 minutes is enough for ~500–600 words
- If you finish early, pause and invite questions rather than padding
- The opening is not a summary of the full paper — it's a *claim* you want to defend
- End with something open: "I'm ready to go deeper on any of these points"
