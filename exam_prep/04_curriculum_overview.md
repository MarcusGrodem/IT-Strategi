## Lecture 1 — What Is Strategy?
- Strategy = positioning (Porter 1996): performing different activities from rivals, or similar activities differently
- Strategy involves assumptions and choices
- Strategy ≠ operations or planning

**Key concept:** Strategic positioning — being different, not just better.

- Tools for analysing the competitive environment
- Typical strategic moves/mechanisms

## Lecture 3 — Strategic Analysis (Part II) + Digital Business Models
- Business model as foundation for strategic position (Weill & Woerner 2018)
- Customer value proposition is central
- Business models can be adjusted and renewed
- Digital business models: more open networks, stronger customer relationships
- **Four basic models: Omnichannel, Supplier, Ecosystem Driver, Modular Producer**
- Business Model Canvas (Osterwalder & Pigneur 2010) — strong analytical tool, not a strategy itself

**Key concepts:** DBM four types, value proposition, BMC.

## Lecture 4 — Strategy and Digital Transformation (Ulrik Røhl)

### Key distinction: Digital Transformation vs. IT-Enabled Organizational Transformation
(Wessel et al. 2021)

| Type | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Digital Transformation** | Digital technology is *central to redefining value propositions*, leading to new organizational identity |
| **IT-Enabled Organizational Transformation** | Digital technology *supports and reinforces an existing* value proposition and identity |

**Why it matters for Coop:** Coop is NOT undergoing digital transformation (it's not redefining its identity around digital). It's doing IT-enabled transformation — using digital to support the store-based business model. The app closure of coop.dk confirms this.

### Wessel et al. (2021) — empirical basis
- Case 1 Alpha: Hospital in France — IT-enabled transformation (EMR system)
- Case 2 Beta: Manufacturing company in Finland — digital transformation ("we used to be machine manufacturers... but now the control software system doesn't need the hardware anymore")

**For exam questions on Lektion 4:** Be able to explain the difference between these two types and give examples. Apply to Coop.

## Lecture 5 — Digital Innovation, Disruption and Strategies (Sebastian Winslow)

### Innovation definitions
- Rogers (1962/1983): "Any idea, practice, or object perceived as new by an individual or unit of adoption"
- Hukal & Henfridsson (2017): "co-creation of novel offerings through recombination of digital and/or physical components"
- Yoo et al. (2010): "carrying out new combinations of digital and physical components to produce novel products"
- Nambisan et al. (2017): "use of digital technology during the process of innovating"

### Digital technology characteristics
- **Data homogeneity** — digital information is the universal resource
- **Editability** — digital content can be changed
- **Self-referential properties** — digital systems can reference and modify themselves

### Recombination
Digital innovation = recombining digital + physical components

### Disruption
Clayton Christensen's disruption theory — incumbent firms focus on high-end customers, disruptors start at low end and move up

**For exam questions on Lektion 5:** Define digital innovation, explain recombination, explain disruption. Example from Coop: Coop App is not disruptive — it's a sustaining innovation that supports the existing business model.

## Exam Prep Tips (from Lektion 9 slides)

From the case competition learnings:
1. Read and answer the full assignment — "go into the case"
2. Be critical of your own answer
3. Be clear and concrete on existing and new value propositions
4. Consider the big picture: industry trends, competitors, new actors
5. Consider dynamic effects: how will customers, competitors, others react?
6. Think beyond yes/no scenarios — be clear in your recommendations
7. Consider what theoretical models can and cannot do — and how to bridge them
