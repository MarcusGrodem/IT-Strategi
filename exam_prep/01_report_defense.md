## Likely Paper-Defense Questions

### "Hvorfor valgte du Martin Hasgard Olesen?"
Martin's domain (communication, marketing, digital) puts him at the intersection of customer-facing app decisions and executive governance. He needs to translate digital activity metrics into financial/strategic language that the executive team understands. The other actors (CEO, chain directors) either own strategy more broadly or are focused on chain operations — Martin is the one who needs to *argue for* the digital investment with credible criteria.

### "Hvorfor hybrid governance og ikke en af de andre muligheder?"
- **Reduce/sell**: Takes financial pressure seriously but risks dismantling a 1.8M-user digital touchpoint and member relationship that's already built.
- **Strengthen**: Takes adoption seriously but risks equating reach with profitability.
- **Sell Lobyco**: The public evidence base is too thin to recommend divestment — we don't know costs, external revenues, or book value.
- **Go deep (separate apps)**: Increases complexity and cost without proof that different apps produce better chain-specific outcomes.
- **Hybrid governance**: Preserves optionality, demands evidence before scaling, links digital to store economics. The most defensible given the source uncertainty.

### "Hvorfor de to teorier — DVC og Digital Business Model?"
They work as a pair. DVC answers: *what kind of digital value can the app create?* DBM answers: *does that value actually fit Coop's business model and get captured financially?* Together they prevent the paper from either over-valuing digital activity (DVC alone) or ignoring digital potential (pure financial lens).

### "Hvad er styrken og svaghederne ved DVC Framework?"
**Strength:** Breaks digital value into granular components (experiences, relationships, relevance, infrastructure, outputs), which lets you diagnose *where* app value may or may not be created.
**Weakness:** Can make digital activity look valuable just because it exists. Doesn't inherently force a financial or strategic discipline question — that's why DBM is needed alongside it.

### "Hvad er styrken og svaghederne ved Digital Business Model-perspektivet (Weill & Woerner)?"
**Strength:** Forces the question of value creation AND value capture. The four business model types (omnichannel, supplier, ecosystem driver, modular producer) give Coop a clear strategic positioning tool.
**Weakness:** The framework describes archetypes — real companies are messier. Coop doesn't fit neatly in one box (store-first omnichannel + potential modular producer through Lobyco). Also, the framework was developed before the current AI/platform era and may not fully capture newer dynamics.

### "Hvad er de centrale kildekritiske problemer?"
The four-level evidence chain:
> Adoption/engagement → customer behaviour → store economics → profitability/strategic advantage

Lobyco, Playable, and Shortcut sources document the first 1–2 levels but **cannot** document levels 3–4. They are commercially interested parties. Without control groups, before/after data, or margin data, you cannot make causal claims about app profitability.

### "Hvorfor anbefalede du ikke at sælge Lobyco?"
The public source material doesn't give enough basis for or against divestment. We don't know: Lobyco's cost structure, its external revenues, its book value, how dependent Coop App is on it, or what a buyer would pay. Recommending divestment based on this would be intellectually dishonest. The right recommendation is to clarify Lobyco's mandate and measure its contribution.

### "Hvad er selektionsbias-problemet med app-data?"
Coop App users may already be Coop's most loyal customers before they download the app. If they shop more often, it might be because they were already loyal — not because the app caused it. Without randomized assignment or natural experiment data, you cannot claim the app *causes* higher loyalty or frequency.

### "Hvad skulle Coop måle (KPI-hierarki)?"
Five levels:
1. Adoption & reach (how many use the app)
2. Engagement (which features, offers, campaigns)
3. Customer behaviour (store visits, redemption, retention, shopping patterns)
4. Store economics (basket size, margin, traffic, friction reduction)
5. Value capture (incremental profit, supplier-funded contribution, retail media net revenue, cost per function)

### "Hvad er research limitations?"
- No internal Coop data (margin, costs, incrementality)
- All app/Lobyco sources are commercially biased
- Selection bias in app-user behaviour data
- Competitor evidence shows correlation, not causation
- Language limitation: paper written in Norwegian (confirm CBS rules)

## Claim-Reason-Evidence Structure (from course)

When answering any question, structure as:
1. **Claim** — what you're asserting
2. **Reason** — why it's true
3. **Evidence** — what data/sources support it
4. **Objections** — what challenges this and your response
