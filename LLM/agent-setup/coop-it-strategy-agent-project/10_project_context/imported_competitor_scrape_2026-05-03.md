## Main Imported Insight

Competitor evidence suggests that stronger competitors do not simply have "more digital" solutions. Their digital tools are stronger when they fit the core business model:

- Salling: scale and store-integrated apps/Scan&Go.
- REMA: discount focus and narrow in-store digital utility.
- Lidl: Lidl Plus reinforces coupons, savings and repeat purchase goals.
- Dagrofa/MENY: loyalty and app/member tools support food/local chain identity.
- Nemlig: online grocery is a purpose-built logistics model, but still has tight bottom-line economics.

## How This Should Affect The Coop Project

This evidence strengthens a source-critical and operationally grounded Coop argument:

> Coop App reach may be valuable, but competitor evidence suggests digital value depends on fit with the store model, chain positioning, customer promise and economics.

This currently strengthens these open strategic positions:

- Hybrid: keep Coop App/Lobyco but tie investment to physical-store economics.
- Differentiate app strategy by chain.

It weakens a simple "strengthen the app significantly" position unless stronger Coop-specific evidence shows financial or store-level value.

## Source Criticism

The scrape output itself is a secondary synthesis. Final paper claims should trace back to original source URLs from `data/source_register.csv`.

Important caveats:

- Company pages and press releases may be promotional.
- App store ratings/downloads do not prove profitability.
- Paqle/Proff are secondary financial databases.
- REMA market-share evidence is contested.
- DI e-commerce data is general market context, not grocery-specific profitability evidence.
- Online grocery evidence from Nemlig should not be used to claim that coop.dk closure was necessarily wrong.
