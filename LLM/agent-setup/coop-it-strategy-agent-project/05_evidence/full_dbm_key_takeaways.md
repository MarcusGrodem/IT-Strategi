# Full DBM PDF — Key Takeaways

Source:

Weill, Peter, and Stephanie L. Woerner. 2018. *What's Your Digital Business Model? Six Questions to Help You Build the Next-Generation Enterprise*. Harvard Business Review Press. Local file: `05_evidence/FULL_DBM.pdf`.

Status:

This is a theory/source takeaway note, not direct Coop evidence. Use it to sharpen the Digital Business Model perspective and to justify which parts of the perspective are applied to Coop App/Lobyco.

## Core Takeaways

### 1. Digital transformation is business model change, not technology adoption

The book frames digital transformation as a shift in how the enterprise creates, delivers and captures value. Technology is an enabler, but not the strategic answer by itself.

Coop application:

- Do not argue that Coop should invest in the app simply because the app is digital or has many users.
- Analyse whether Coop App and Lobyco change Coop's value proposition, customer relationship, value capture or operating model.
- Strong wording: app reach is an input to business model analysis, not proof of business model success.

### 2. Digital threat should be assessed before choosing the future model

Weill and Woerner start with the question of how much of the current business model is under digital threat. The point is to create urgency and a shared view of what must change.

Coop application:

- Coop's threat is not only online grocery disruption. It is also loss of customer relationship, price/value pressure, stronger competitor app utilities and weak profitability.
- The threat assessment should connect to Coop's current facts: store-based core, closed online grocery/webshop channels and financial pressure.
- Avoid generic "digital disruption" paragraphs unless they are tied to concrete Coop threats.

### 3. The DBM framework has two axes: customer knowledge and business design

The framework asks:

- how much the enterprise knows about end customers, including identity, purchase history, interactions, goals and decision process;
- whether the business is organized as a controlled value chain or as part of a broader ecosystem.

The four resulting models are supplier, omnichannel, modular producer and ecosystem driver.

Coop application:

- Coop App and the membership base may move Coop upward on customer knowledge, but only if data is usable for better customer/store decisions.
- Coop's physical stores and chain portfolio look more like an omnichannel/store-linked value-chain logic than a full ecosystem-driver position.
- Lobyco could be analysed as a possible modular producer capability, but only if its plug-and-play/OEM claims and external value are documented.

### 4. Multiple business models can coexist, but they need clear synergies

The book notes that large enterprises often operate in more than one model. That is acceptable when the models reinforce each other or deliberately diversify risk.

Coop application:

- Coop may simultaneously be a store-based retailer, a membership/loyalty operator and a possible Lobyco platform/OEM owner.
- The analytical question is whether these roles reinforce Coop's core business or create unfocused complexity.
- This supports a governance recommendation: keep, expand or reduce Lobyco only after clarifying its contribution to Coop's store economics and member relationship.

### 5. Ecosystem driver is attractive but hard to justify for Coop

Ecosystem drivers are described as strong performers because they become the customer's destination in a domain, integrate partners and collect data across interactions. But the model is difficult and requires strong content, customer experience and platforms.

Coop application:

- Be careful with platform/ecosystem language. Coop has supplier campaigns, app users and Lobyco claims, but public evidence does not prove that Coop is a mature ecosystem driver.
- Retail media and supplier-funded gamification can be discussed as ecosystem-like mechanisms, not as proof of ecosystem-driver status.
- A safer frame is that Coop should selectively buy options toward ecosystem capabilities where they clearly strengthen the store/member model.

### 6. Competitive advantage comes from content, customer experience and platforms

The book uses three digital competitive-advantage sources:

- content: products, services and information;
- customer experience: multichannel/multiproduct experience and customer-facing processes;
- platforms: shared digitized processes, data and infrastructure.

Coop application:

- Coop's content includes groceries, private labels, chain promises, offers, recipes and member benefits.
- Coop's customer experience includes app functions, Scan & Betal, offers, games, receipts, local store content and store journey.
- Coop's platform capability includes Lobyco, member data, personalization, APIs/OEM potential and reusable app infrastructure.
- The final paper should avoid trying to make Coop world-class in all three at once. Under financial pressure, Coop needs a prioritized advantage.

### 7. Mobile strategy must match the business model

The book presents mobile as a way to connect and learn, but stresses that there is no one-size-fits-all model. Mobile apps can support brand enhancement, multichannel integration, B2B2C access, targeted segments or mobile-first innovation.

Coop application:

- Coop App should be evaluated as a mobile connection layer between member, store, chain and supplier campaign.
- The most relevant mobile strategies for Coop are multichannel/store integration and targeted segment or chain-specific propositions.
- A pure mobile-first or platform-first argument is weaker unless Lobyco economics and external customers can be documented.

### 8. Capabilities matter as much as vision

The book lists eight capabilities for digital reinvention, including customer information, customer voice, evidence-based decisions, integrated customer experience, distinctiveness, partnerships, service-enabled core capabilities/APIs, and efficiency/security/compliance.

Coop application:

- The strongest direct fit is evidence-based decision making: Coop should require app/Lobyco KPIs that separate engagement from incremental business value.
- Customer information and customer voice matter because Coop has membership data but must turn it into better offers, store experience and chain relevance.
- Service-enabled core capabilities are relevant if Lobyco is treated as reusable infrastructure, not just an app vendor.
- Efficiency and compliance matter because Coop is financially pressured and handles customer data.

### 9. Digital reinvention requires ambidexterity: innovate and cut costs

Weill and Woerner argue that top performers must innovate while also reducing cost and simplifying operations.

Coop application:

- This is highly relevant to Coop: the app/Lobyco question should not be framed as "invest more" versus "cut digital".
- Stronger recommendation logic: fund the app/Lobyco features that create measurable store/member value, while pruning features and complexity that do not.
- This supports stage-gated investment governance rather than a broad digital growth narrative.

### 10. The framework is useful because it creates a shared language for choices

The book's six questions are less a checklist for a long theory section and more a decision language for leadership.

Coop application:

- Use DBM selectively: threat, target model, advantage, mobile connection, capabilities and leadership/governance.
- The final paper should apply these questions to Coop's concrete dilemma rather than explaining the whole book.
- DBM should support recommendations, not dominate the report.

## Best Coop-Specific Use

Use Weill & Woerner to support this core argument:

> Coop App and Lobyco should be evaluated as business model components, not as standalone digital success stories. Their strategic value depends on whether they increase customer knowledge, strengthen the store/member value proposition, create measurable value capture and build capabilities Coop can govern under financial pressure.

## Source-Critical Note

This is a reputable management/theory source, but it is not Coop-specific evidence. It contains survey-based and case-based claims from before 2018. Use it for conceptual framing and analytical questions, not as proof that any specific Coop action will work in 2026.
