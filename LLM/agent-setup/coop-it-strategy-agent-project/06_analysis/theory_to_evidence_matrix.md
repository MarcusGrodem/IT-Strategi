# Theory To Evidence Matrix

Purpose: test which course perspectives fit the evidence already collected. This file does not lock the final theory choice.

Assignment rule:

> Recommendations must be based on 1-2 self-selected course perspectives. The paper may use selected parts of a perspective if the selection is well justified and transparent.

Therefore, this matrix should help choose not only "which theory", but also which parts of the theory are actually useful for the Coop case.

## Matrix

| Theory / perspective | Evidence it explains well | Evidence it explains poorly | What it can help argue | Main limitation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| DVC Framework | Coop App features, app engagement claims, customer experience, relationships, relevance, digital infrastructure, competitor app utilities | Financial performance, ownership/governance of Lobyco, broader business model economics | Whether Coop App creates digital value for customers and relationships | Can become too positive about digital value unless combined with source criticism and financial discipline |
| Digital Business Model perspective | Lobyco as capability, retail media, app as part of value logic, store-based economics after coop.dk closure, competitor business-model fit | Detailed customer experience mechanisms | Whether Coop App/Lobyco creates a viable value logic beyond engagement | Requires precise framing; otherwise it becomes abstract |
| Platform / ecosystem perspective | Lobyco, suppliers, retail media, app users, campaigns and data-mediated interactions | Store execution, price competition, financial pressure | Whether Coop can use Lobyco/app to connect customers, stores and suppliers | May overstate Coop as a mature platform |
| Porter / positioning | Competitor evidence, discount pressure, chain identity, value-for-money, differentiation | Internal digital capability and app mechanics | Whether Coop's digital strategy supports a clear competitive position | Less digital-specific; may broaden the paper too much |
| Implementation perspective | Store integration, Scan&Go, coupon mechanics, online grocery logistics, KPI/governance discipline | Customer meaning and digital value concepts | Whether Coop can operationalise app/Lobyco value under financial pressure | Can become execution-heavy and less strategic/theoretical |
| Resource-based view | Lobyco as possible rare digital capability, Coop App reach, internal competence | Customer-facing value and competitor positioning | Whether Lobyco should be kept as a strategic asset | Must be supported with evidence that the capability is valuable, rare and hard to imitate |

## Best-Fit Theory Combinations

| DVC + Porter/Positioning | Customer/app value plus competitor and chain positioning | Strong if competitor angle dominates | May become broader than digital strategy | Medium/High |

## Current Interpretation

The evidence currently points most strongly toward a theory combination that can handle both:

1. digital customer value, and
2. fit with business model, stores, chains and economics.

Therefore, the strongest current candidate remains:

> DVC Framework + Digital Business Model perspective

But this is not locked. If the final paper focuses more on competitor positioning, DVC + Porter/positioning may become stronger. If retail media and supplier value become central, DVC + Platform/Ecosystem may become stronger.
