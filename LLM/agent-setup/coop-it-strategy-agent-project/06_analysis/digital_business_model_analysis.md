# Digital Business Model Analysis — Starter

This is a starter analysis. It tests how a Digital Business Model perspective could be applied to Coop App/Lobyco.

## Why This Perspective Fits

The case is not only about whether customers like the Coop App. It is about whether Coop App and Lobyco form a strategically meaningful value logic under Coop's current conditions.

Important case conditions:

- Coop is financially pressured.
- Coop closed coop.dk and currently depends on physical stores.
- Lobyco may represent a digital capability beyond a normal loyalty app.
- Retail media and supplier-funded campaigns may create a different value logic.
- Competitor evidence suggests digital value depends on fit with business model and operations.

## Business Model Questions

| Question | Why it matters for Coop |
|---|---|
| Who receives value? | Customers, stores, Coop chains, suppliers, Lobyco and Coop ownership may receive different forms of value |
| What is the value proposition? | Savings, convenience, loyalty, inspiration, store flow, data, campaigns or retail media |
| What resources/capabilities matter? | App reach, data, Lobyco competence, store network, chain brands, supplier relations |
| How is value captured? | Store traffic, basket size, loyalty, supplier-funded campaigns, retail media revenue, cost savings |
| What is the operating fit? | Digital tools must fit physical stores after coop.dk closure |
| What is the governance problem? | Further investment should depend on KPIs and strategic contribution |

## Strong Digital Business Model Argument

Claim:

> Coop App and Lobyco should be evaluated as part of Coop's business model, not as standalone digital success stories.

Reasoning:

Coop's current business model is store-based and financially pressured. The app may create value only if it improves physical-store economics, strengthens chain propositions or enables credible supplier-funded value without damaging trust.

Empirical observations:

- Coop closed coop.dk and currently sells through physical stores.
- Coop has official 2025 results showing weak operating profitability.
- Competitors show different forms of digital fit: Salling with store utility, Lidl with discount coupons, REMA with simple Scan Selv, Dagrofa with food/local identity and Nemlig with logistics-heavy online grocery.

Strategic implication:

Coop should govern further app/Lobyco investment through a business-model logic:

- Which chain/customer problem does the app solve?
- Which store-level metric improves?
- Which supplier/customer value is created?
- Which features should be reduced if they do not support the model?

## Limitations

This perspective may underplay customer experience and relationship quality if used alone. It works best with DVC, which explains how digital value is experienced by customers.
