# Strategic Situation Analysis

This is the central analysis base for the exam paper. It is not a final report and it does not choose the advised executive, theory combination or strategic stance.

Official assignment logic:

```text
I) Analyse Coop's strategic situation
II) On that basis, develop 2-5 relevant strategic recommendations
```

This file therefore prepares the later recommendation work by clarifying what the evidence currently suggests, what remains uncertain and how different theoretical lenses can be used analytically.

## Strategic Question

How should Coop understand its strategic situation in 2026 when deciding the future of Coop App and Lobyco, given financial pressure, physical-store dependence, chain differences, competitor pressure and uncertainty in the available evidence?

## Short Introduction To The Strategic Situation

Coop's strategic situation is shaped by a tension between a visible digital asset and a pressured core business. The case material treats Coop as financially pressured, store-based after the closure of coop.dk and engaged in a strategic discussion about whether the Coop App and Lobyco should be reduced, sold, strengthened, differentiated by chain or governed differently. Evidence entries E001-E003, E006-E008 and E016 support this as the factual foundation, while E004, E009-E011 and the source criticism files warn that app success claims are often commercially framed.

The key strategic issue is therefore not whether digital tools matter in grocery retail. Competitor evidence indicates that they do (E015, E018, E021, E026). The sharper issue is whether Coop App and Lobyco create value that fits Coop's store-based economics, chain architecture and investment constraints. This analysis keeps the case option space open for later evaluation:

- reduce app investment,
- sell or partly divest Lobyco,
- strengthen app investment,
- go deep with different chain app solutions,
- hybrid governance,
- selective simplification.

The analysis uses theory as a diagnostic tool rather than as generic theory summary. DVC is used to test digital customer value; the Digital Business Model perspective is used to test value creation, value capture and operating fit; Porter/positioning is used where competitor fit and chain trade-offs matter.

## 1. Financial Pressure And Investment Scarcity

Claim:

> Coop's app/Lobyco decision should be analysed as an investment allocation problem, not only as a digital ambition.

Reasoning:

The case and evidence register indicate that Coop is financially pressured (E003, E006-E008, E016). Under those conditions, continued app development has an opportunity cost: resources allocated to Coop App or Lobyco cannot simultaneously be allocated to store operations, pricing, formats, logistics, communication or chain-specific renewal. A high-reach app may still be strategically weak if it absorbs scarce investment without demonstrable contribution to store economics or chain positioning.

Empirical observations:

| Observation | Claim type | Evidence link | Analytical use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Coop is financially pressured | Fact | E003, E006 | Establishes investment scarcity |
| The evidence register includes revenue decline and loss figures from the exam context | Fact pending exact wording | E007, E008 | Supports caution around major new investments |
| Coop official 2025 results release reports 32.5bn DKK revenue, EBITDA of 313m DKK and EBIT loss of 215m DKK | Fact | E016 | Shows weak operating profitability even if exact profit/loss metric must be precise |
| App adoption and engagement claims do not automatically prove profitability | Inference | E013; claim reliability matrix | Prevents app metrics from being treated as investment justification |

Theory link:

- Digital Business Model: The relevant test is not "is the app active?", but whether digital value creation turns into value capture through store traffic, basket effects, supplier-funded value, cost savings or stronger chain economics.
- DVC Framework: Digital outputs such as adoption and engagement are only intermediate outputs. They need to be connected to outcomes.
- Porter/positioning: Investment choices are trade-offs. If Coop tries to invest heavily in every possible digital feature while also fixing price, stores and chain positioning, the activity system may become incoherent.

Strategic implication:

Later recommendations should include investment discipline regardless of the final stance. Even a "strengthen app investment" stance would need stronger KPI logic; a "reduce" or "sell" stance would need to explain what higher-priority investment receives the freed resources. This theme does not decide the stance, but it raises the burden of proof for broad app expansion.

## 2. Bricks-And-Mortar As The Strategic Core

Claim:

> Coop App should be analysed as a store-linked digital asset because Coop's current grocery model is anchored in physical stores.

Reasoning:

The exam case states that Coop closed coop.dk in 2023 because it was not profitable and that Coop currently sells through physical stores (E001, E002). This means the app should not be evaluated as a general e-commerce transformation. Its strategic value depends on whether it improves the physical shopping journey, loyalty to the chain, store flow, local relevance, campaign conversion or supplier/customer interaction around stores.

Empirical observations:

| Observation | Claim type | Evidence link | Analytical use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Coop closed coop.dk in 2023 due to lack of profitability | Fact | E001 | Indicates limits of online grocery for Coop |
| Coop sells only through physical stores today | Fact | E002 | Makes store impact central |
| Competitor evidence suggests Salling uses app/Scan&Go mechanics as store utility | Feature claim / inference | E018; competitor evidence | Indicates a store-integrated digital logic |
| Nemlig evidence shows online grocery may grow but still has difficult bottom-line economics | Mixed claim / inference | E025 | Prevents a simplistic "online equals future" argument |

Theory link:

- Digital Business Model: The channel logic matters. Coop App is not a substitute webshop; it is a digital layer on a store-based model.
- DVC Framework: App experiences, relationships and relevance should be assessed in relation to the store visit and membership relationship.
- Porter/positioning: The app should reinforce the chosen competitive position of each chain rather than create a detached digital identity.

Strategic implication:

The later analysis should ask whether each app feature supports physical-store economics. Features that create engagement without improving the store model should be treated cautiously. This supports hybrid governance and selective simplification as serious option categories, while still leaving room for stronger app investment if the investment is store-linked.

## 3. Coop App And Lobyco As Digital Capacity

Claim:

> Coop App and Lobyco may represent a strategic digital capacity, but their value depends on governance, contribution to the Coop business model and evidence of outcomes.

Reasoning:

The case/source base claims that Coop App was developed internally and later separated into Lobyco (E005). This may indicate that Coop has built a digital capability that could be valuable through app development, customer data, loyalty infrastructure, retail media and supplier campaigns. However, Lobyco, Shortcut and Playable are commercially interested or supplier/developer sources. Their claims are useful for describing intended value and capabilities, but not for proving strategic or financial success.

Empirical observations:

| Observation | Claim type | Evidence link | Analytical use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Coop App was originally developed internally and later separated into Lobyco | Claim | E005 | Supports treating Lobyco as possible capability |
| Lobyco presents Coop App as successful through adoption/engagement metrics | Marketing claim | E004 | Shows claimed digital reach, not proven value |
| Shortcut presents development history and feature capability | Marketing claim | E011 | Useful for capability description, not neutral evaluation |
| Playable presents gamification as campaign engagement/retail media value | Marketing claim | E010 | Useful for intended value logic, not proof of profit |
| Retail media/supplier-funded campaigns may create value but require governance | Claim / inference | claim reliability matrix; source criticism | Identifies possible value capture with trust and causality risks |

Theory link:

- Digital Business Model: Lobyco should be tested as a key resource/capability and possible value-capture mechanism, not simply as a cost or success story.
- DVC Framework: Lobyco can support digital infrastructure, experiences, relationships and outputs, but the framework also requires asking what value is actually created.
- Platform/ecosystem perspective may become relevant later if retail media and supplier interaction become central, but current evidence is not yet strong enough to treat Coop as a mature platform.

Strategic implication:

The "sell Lobyco" and "keep/strengthen Lobyco" options both remain open. The analysis should later compare whether Lobyco is a capability Coop cannot easily recreate, a costly distraction from core retail turnaround, or an asset that should be governed more tightly through business-unit needs and measurable outcomes.

## 4. App Reach Versus Strategic And Economic Value

Claim:

> Coop App reach is strategically relevant, but it should not be treated as proof of loyalty, profitability or competitive advantage.

Reasoning:

High adoption, household reach, engagement, campaign participation or frequent use may indicate that Coop has customer access and digital relevance. However, source criticism warns that adoption and engagement do not automatically imply profitable behaviour. App users may already be more loyal Coop customers, meaning some claims about higher shopping frequency could reflect selection bias rather than app-caused behaviour.

Empirical observations:

| Observation | Claim type | Evidence link | Analytical use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lobyco claims app success through adoption or engagement | Marketing claim | E004 | Indicates claimed reach |
| Lobyco claims app users shop more frequently or are more engaged | Marketing claim | E009 | Useful but high causality risk |
| App adoption and engagement do not automatically prove profitability | Inference | E013; source criticism | Central safeguard against overclaiming |
| DI e-commerce data indicates mobile/digital purchasing behaviour is mainstream | Market statistic | E026 | Supports digital relevance generally, not grocery profitability specifically |

Theory link:

- DVC Framework: Reach and engagement belong under digital outputs and relationships. They are necessary indicators but insufficient as strategic outcomes.
- Digital Business Model: The missing link is value capture. The app must support revenue, margin, supplier value, retention, lower costs or stronger chain positioning.
- Porter/positioning: If competitors can replicate app features, reach alone may not create sustainable differentiation.

Strategic implication:

Later recommendations should separate metric levels:

```text
Adoption/engagement -> customer behaviour -> store economics -> profitability/strategic advantage
```

This theme weakens any final argument that simply says Coop should invest more because the app is popular. It also weakens a simple reduction argument if the app reach is one of Coop's few scalable customer assets. The useful conclusion is conditional: app reach matters if it can be converted into relevant, measurable business outcomes.

## 5. Chain Differences And The "Go Deep" Possibility

Claim:

> Coop's chain diversity suggests that one shared app logic may not be equally valuable for all chains.

Reasoning:

The case explicitly asks whether Coop should "go deep" by developing significantly different app solutions for different chains. Coop's relevant executives also represent different chain or functional priorities: group-level trade-offs, SuperBrugsen/Kvickly, Brugsen, 365discount and communication/marketing/digital. Competitor evidence indicates that digital tools create more strategic value when they fit a clear customer promise and operating model.

Empirical observations:

| Observation | Claim type | Evidence link | Analytical use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Salling uses chain-specific apps and store-integrated utility | Feature claim / inference | E018; competitor evidence | Supports chain/app fit logic |
| Lidl Plus reinforces discount behaviour through offers, coupons and purchase goals | Marketing claim / feature claim | E021 | Indicates discount app value may be savings-oriented |
| REMA evidence points to focused discount and narrower digital utility such as Scan Selv | Inference | E019 | Suggests simplicity can beat feature breadth |
| Dagrofa/MENY evidence suggests loyalty can come from food/local identity | Inference | E024 | Supports app value tied to chain identity |
| Loyalty Group/BrancheIndex claims are useful but methodology and promotional framing must be checked | Claim with caution | competitor evidence; source criticism | Prevents overclaiming brand rankings |

Theory link:

- DVC Framework: Relevance is central. Different customers and shopping missions may require different digital experiences and relationships.
- Porter/positioning: Chain-specific fit and trade-offs matter. A discount chain and a local/community chain may need different activity systems.
- Digital Business Model: Different chains may have different value propositions and value-capture mechanisms, even if they share technical infrastructure.

Strategic implication:

"Go deep" is analytically plausible but must be separated into at least two versions:

- full separate apps or major chain-specific app experiences,
- shared platform with chain-specific modules, KPIs and governance.

The second version may preserve scale benefits while addressing chain fit. The analysis does not yet decide whether this is worth the added complexity.

## 6. Competitor Pressure And Digital Fit

Claim:

> Competitor evidence suggests that Coop should not ask whether competitors are "more digital", but whether their digital tools fit their core business models better.

Reasoning:

The imported competitor evidence does not point to one universal winning digital model. Salling, REMA, Lidl, Dagrofa/MENY and Nemlig appear to use digital tools differently. The strongest current inference is that digital value is created when tools reinforce a clear operating model: scale and store utility, discount savings, local/food identity or dedicated online logistics.

Strategic implication:

## 7. Source Uncertainty, Marketing Bias And Assumptions

## 8. Importert Coop Business Model Evidence: Bricks-And-Mortar, Chain Complexity And Digital Value Logic

Claim:

> Den importerte Coop-evidensen styrker bildet av Coop som en fysisk dagligvareaktør med en sterk digital kundekontakt, men den dokumenterer ikke sikkert at appen, Lobyco, retail media eller gamification skaper netto økonomisk verdi.

Reasoning:

Årsrapporttall og offisielle Coop-kilder gir sterkere dokumentasjon på Coop sin skala, butikkavhengighet og medlemsmodell enn tidligere handoff-notater. Derfor må analysen holde to tanker samtidig: Coop har en reell digital reach/capability, men strategisk verdi må vurderes gjennom fysisk butikkøkonomi, kjedefit og governance.

- **Digital Business Model:** Den nye evidensen gjør value capture-spørsmålet mer presist: Coop må koble appreach til butikktrafikk, kurv, margin, leverandørfinansierte kampanjer, retail media, data og Lobyco-økonomi.
- **DVC Framework:** Coop App kan analyseres gjennom experiences, relationships, digital infrastructure, outputs og relevance. Men DVC-output som reach eller engagement må ikke forveksles med strategisk outcome.
- **Porter/positioning:** Kjedeporteføljen viser flere posisjoner samtidig: discount, kvalitet/lokal mat, community og bredt varehus. Appen må støtte disse aktivitetssystemene, ikke bare være en felles digital flate.
- **Implementation/governance:** Spørsmålet blir hvordan Coop styrer appinvesteringer med KPI-er som skiller adoption fra inkrementell verdi.

Strategic implication:

Den nye evidensen styrker tre åpne stances uten å velge mellom dem:

1. **Hybrid governance** blir mer relevant fordi den kan kombinere felles app/Lobyco-infrastruktur med krav til kjede- og butikkverdi.
2. **Kjedespesifikk modulering / “go deep”** blir mer relevant fordi Coop-kjedene faktisk uttrykker ulike kundeløfter.
3. **Porteføljedisciplin rundt Lobyco** blir mer relevant fordi Lobyco kan være både digital kapabilitet og mulig distraksjon fra butikkturnaround.

Den nye evidensen svekker samtidig en enkel “styrk appen fordi den er populær”-argumentasjon. Vendor-tall støtter app-adopsjon og engagement, men ikke nødvendigvis lønnsomhet. Den svekker også en enkel “kutt digitalt”-argumentasjon, fordi Coop faktisk har en stor digital kundekontakt som kan være vanskelig å erstatte.

Kritisk formuleringsregel:

> Skriv at kildene indikerer mulig digital verdi og mulig supplier-/retail-media-logikk. Ikke trekk bastante konklusjoner om appens lønnsomhet eller Lobyco-eierskap fra disse kildene alene.

## Provisional Analytical Conclusion

Coop's strategic situation is not a simple choice between "digital" and "physical". The current evidence suggests a more precise problem:

> Coop must decide whether Coop App and Lobyco can be governed as digital assets that strengthen physical-store economics, chain-specific customer promises and value capture under financial pressure.

This statement is an analytical inference. It does not prove that Coop should invest more, reduce investment, sell Lobyco or go deep by chain. It indicates which tests the later recommendation work must pass:

- Does the option fit Coop's store-based business model?
- Does it address financial pressure and opportunity cost?
- Does it convert app reach into measurable business value?
- Does it respect chain differences?
- Does it use Lobyco as capability only where that capability creates value?
- Does it avoid relying on marketing claims as facts?

## What This Analysis Does Not Yet Decide

This analysis deliberately leaves the following choices open:

- It does not choose the advised executive. All five case actors remain possible.
- It does not choose the final theory perspective. DVC, Digital Business Model and Porter/positioning remain candidates, with other perspectives possible if later evidence supports them.
- It does not choose a final strategic stance on Coop App or Lobyco.
- It does not decide whether Lobyco should be sold, retained or strengthened.
- It does not decide whether Coop should build separate chain apps or use a shared app with chain-specific modules.
- It does not decide the final 2-5 recommendations.
- It does not treat Lobyco, Playable or Shortcut marketing claims as proof of profitability.

Before final recommendations are written, the project should still verify exact source wording for important financial and app-performance claims, decide which competitor evidence is worth using, choose the advised executive and choose 1-2 course perspectives that best support the final argument.
