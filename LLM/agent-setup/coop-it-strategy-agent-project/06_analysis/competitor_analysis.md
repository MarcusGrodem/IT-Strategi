## Purpose

The competitor analysis should answer:

> Does Coop's strategic challenge primarily concern digital weakness, price/store-format competition, loyalty, physical-store execution, or a combination?

## Working Hypotheses

These are evidence-informed hypotheses, not final conclusions.

| Hypothesis | What would support it | What would weaken it | Strategic implication for Coop |
|---|---|---|---|
| H1: Discount competitors pressure Coop mainly through price and simplicity | Evidence of discount growth, strong price positioning and simple store formats | Evidence that competitor apps/loyalty are the main driver | Coop App must support savings and simplicity, not just engagement |
| H2: Grocery apps are becoming hygiene factors rather than differentiators | Similar loyalty/app features across competitors | Evidence that Coop App has unique, hard-to-copy effects | Coop should not overstate app uniqueness |
| H3: Coop's chain diversity requires differentiated digital value | Different customer needs across 365discount, Kvickly, SuperBrugsen and other formats | Evidence that one shared app logic works equally well across chains | App strategy should differ by chain and shopping mission |
| H4: Retail media may create new supplier-funded value | Evidence that retail media is growing in grocery and suppliers pay for targeted access | Evidence of weak supplier uptake or customer trust risks | Coop should develop retail media carefully with trust safeguards |
| H5: coop.dk closure makes physical-store impact the key digital KPI | Evidence that Coop now sells only through stores and online grocery was unprofitable | Evidence that online grocery is becoming essential and profitable for competitors | App/Lobyco should be evaluated through store economics |

## Exam-Relevant Interpretation

The competitor evidence supports a balanced strategic argument:

> Coop's challenge is not whether digital tools matter. They do. The issue is whether Coop App and Lobyco are governed in a way that strengthens Coop's store-based economics, chain positioning and customer promise.

This evidence strengthens these open strategic positions:

- Hybrid: keep Coop App/Lobyco but tie investment to physical-store economics.
- Differentiate app strategy by chain.

It weakens a simple "strengthen app significantly" position unless stronger evidence shows the Coop App creates financial or store-level value.

It also weakens a simple "sell Lobyco" position unless evidence later shows Lobyco distracts from Coop's core turnaround or cannot create relevant store/customer value.
