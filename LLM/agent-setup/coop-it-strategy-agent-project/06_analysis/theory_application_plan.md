## Purpose

This file explains how the selected perspectives should be applied to the Coop case.

The assignment allows using selected parts of broader course perspectives, as long as the selection is justified and transparent.

## Overall Logic

The two perspectives should work together:

| Perspective | Main analytical role |
|---|---|
| DVC Framework | Analyse what kinds of digital value Coop App can create for customers, relationships, relevance and digital infrastructure |
| Digital Business Model perspective | Analyse whether this digital value fits Coop's store-based business model, value capture, Lobyco capability and investment governance |

Short version:

> DVC explains possible digital value. Digital Business Model explains whether that value fits Coop's business model and can support strategic recommendations.

## DVC: Parts To Use

| DVC part | Why relevant to Coop | How to apply |
|---|---|---|
| Experiences | Coop App may improve shopping through offers, Scan & Pay, games, recipes or convenience | Assess whether app features solve real customer/store problems |
| Relationships | Coop App and membership can support loyalty, repeated contact and personal offers | Analyse whether relationship claims indicate value or merely engagement |
| Relevance | Coop has different chains and customer missions | Test whether one app logic fits all chains or whether chain-specific relevance is needed |
| Digital infrastructure | Lobyco and Coop App may represent a digital capability | Analyse whether infrastructure supports Coop's physical-store model |
| Digital outputs | App users, engagement, campaigns and retail media metrics are outputs | Separate adoption/output metrics from strategic and financial outcomes |

## DVC: Parts To Keep Brief Or Avoid

| DVC part / use | Reason |
|---|---|
| Generic digital transformation discussion | Too broad and not directly tied to case question |
| Uncritical success language | App adoption does not prove profitability |
| Long theory explanation | Exam rewards application, not model description |

## Digital Business Model: Parts To Use

| Business model part | Why relevant to Coop | How to apply |
|---|---|---|
| Value proposition | Coop App may promise savings, convenience, relevance, loyalty and inspiration | Clarify what value the app should create for different chains/customers |
| Value creation | Value may be created for customers, stores, Coop chains, suppliers and Lobyco | Analyse who benefits and how |
| Value capture | Coop must capture value through store traffic, basket size, loyalty, retail media, supplier campaigns or cost efficiency | Test whether app claims connect to actual business outcomes |
| Key resources/capabilities | Coop App, Lobyco, member data, store network and supplier relations may be strategic resources | Analyse whether Lobyco should be kept, governed, reduced or possibly divested |
| Channels | Coop is store-based after coop.dk closure | Analyse the app as a store-linked digital channel, not a replacement webshop |
| Governance/KPIs | App investment creates opportunity cost under financial pressure | Recommend measurement and investment discipline later |

## Digital Business Model: Parts To Keep Brief Or Avoid

| Part / use | Reason |
|---|---|
| Full business model canvas | Likely too broad for 5-25 pages |
| Abstract platform scaling logic | Risk of overstating Lobyco/platform evidence |
| Claims about profitability without evidence | The model can structure analysis but cannot prove financial outcomes alone |

## How The Theories Connect To The Strategic Situation Analysis

| Strategic situation theme | DVC contribution | Digital Business Model contribution |
|---|---|---|
| Financial pressure | Shows that engagement alone is insufficient | Frames app/Lobyco as investment and value-capture question |
| Bricks-and-mortar core | Analyses app experience in store context | Frames app as channel supporting physical stores |
| App reach vs strategic value | Distinguishes digital outputs from deeper value | Tests whether reach becomes value capture |
| Chain differences | Uses relevance to test chain-specific needs | Tests whether different chains need different value propositions |
| Lobyco | Treats it as digital infrastructure/capability | Tests capability, governance, value capture and possible divestment logic |
| Retail media | Treats campaigns as digital outputs/relationships | Tests supplier-funded value and trust/governance risks |
| Competitor fit | Shows different digital customer experiences | Shows digital fit with business model and customer promise |

## How The Theories Should Lead To Recommendations

The final recommendations should follow this chain:

```text
Evidence -> DVC interpretation -> Digital Business Model interpretation -> strategic implication -> recommendation
```

Example:

```text
Evidence:
Coop App has reach, but app/vendor claims do not prove profitability.

DVC interpretation:
The app may create relationship and relevance value, but output metrics must not be confused with outcomes.

Digital Business Model interpretation:
Further investment should be governed by value capture through store traffic, basket size, supplier-funded value or chain-specific performance.

Strategic implication:
Coop should not simply strengthen or cut the app. It needs investment governance and chain-specific value tests.

Possible recommendation:
Introduce app/Lobyco KPI governance tied to physical-store economics and chain-specific value propositions.
```

## Required Wording Discipline

Use:

- "DVC helps analyse..."
- "Digital Business Model perspective helps assess..."
- "The evidence indicates..."
- "The source claims..."
- "This should be treated cautiously..."
- "This supports possible value, but not proof of profitability..."

Avoid:

- "DVC proves..."
- "The app is profitable..."
- "Lobyco is definitely a competitive advantage..."
- "Digital transformation is important..." without case-specific meaning.
