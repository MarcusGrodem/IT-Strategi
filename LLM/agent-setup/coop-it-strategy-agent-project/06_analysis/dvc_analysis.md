# DVC Analysis — Starter

This is a starter analysis. It tests how the Digital Value Creation Framework could be applied to Coop App/Lobyco.

## DVC Fit

The DVC Framework is useful because the case is about whether digital initiatives create value for customers, relationships and the organisation.

The framework should be applied directly to Coop. It should not become a long generic theory section.

## DVC Application Matrix

| DVC concept | Coop observation | Supporting evidence | Analytical meaning | Strategic implication |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Experiences | Coop App can support offers, shopping support, Scan & Pay, games or other app-based interactions | Lobyco/Shortcut/Playable claims; source criticism needed | The app may improve shopping experience, but experience value must solve real customer problems | Evaluate features by customer utility and store impact, not novelty |
| Relationships | Coop App and membership can support loyalty, personal offers and repeated customer contact | Lobyco claims; competitor evidence from Lidl Plus, MENY, SPAR | App-based relationships may be valuable, but loyalty claims can reflect selection bias | Avoid treating app-user engagement as proof of profitable loyalty |
| Relevance | Coop has multiple chains with different customer needs | Competitor evidence from REMA, Lidl, Dagrofa/MENY and Salling | One app logic may not fit discount, local, supermarket and premium/food missions equally | Consider chain-specific app value propositions |
| Digital infrastructure | Lobyco and Coop App represent digital capability and infrastructure | Exam case, Lobyco/Shortcut claims | Infrastructure may be a strategic asset if it supports stores, data use and supplier value | Keep capability only if governed by clear business value |
| Digital outputs | App adoption, campaign engagement and retail media metrics may be outputs | Lobyco/Playable marketing claims | Outputs can show activity but not necessarily profit or strategic advantage | Separate output metrics from outcome metrics |
| Evolution | Coop moved from internal app development to Lobyco and closed coop.dk | Exam case and source material | Coop's digital model has evolved from online store ambition toward app/store model | Analyse digital strategy as a store-linked model, not generic online transformation |

## Strong DVC Argument

Claim:

> Coop App can create digital value, but only if its experiences, relationships and relevance are tied to Coop's store-based model and different chain promises.

Reasoning:

The case indicates that Coop no longer sells groceries online through coop.dk and is financially pressured. Competitor evidence shows that digital tools work best when they reinforce a clear business model.

Empirical observations:

- Coop is store-based after coop.dk closure.
- Lobyco claims strong app reach and engagement.
- Lidl Plus reinforces discount value through coupons and purchase goals.
- Salling integrates Scan&Go/store utility into chain apps.
- Dagrofa/MENY suggests loyalty can come from clear food/local positioning.

Strategic implication:

The app should not be evaluated mainly by adoption or engagement. It should be evaluated by whether it improves customer relevance, store traffic, chain positioning and economically meaningful outcomes.

## DVC Limitations

DVC is strong for analysing digital value creation, but weak for proving profitability. It needs support from:

- source criticism,
- business model thinking,
- competitor evidence,
- financial pressure and KPI logic.
