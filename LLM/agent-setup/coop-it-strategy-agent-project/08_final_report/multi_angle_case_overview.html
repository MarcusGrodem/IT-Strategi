Coop IT-Strategy Exam Project · 2026-05-04

Multi-Angle Case Overview

A red-team and option-mapping report that lays out possible directions the Coop App / Lobyco case can be taken. The purpose is to help the user weigh angles before locking executive, theory or strategic position.

About this report

The exam assignment asks the project to I) analyse Coop's strategic situation and II) on that basis develop 2-5 strategic recommendations that prepare one named member of Coop's executive management for the expected discussion about Coop App and Lobyco.

This report does not produce final recommendations. It maps possible angles per case actor, identifies what each angle requires in the situation analysis, and surfaces evidence, theory fit, source-use questions and exam risks. The goal is to keep alternatives open while sharpening the basis for later choice.

Reading instruction. Each angle is a hypothesis, not a recommendation. Language is deliberately cautious: "claims", "indicates", "suggests", "should be treated cautiously". Marketing-derived claims (Lobyco, Playable, Shortcut) are treated as claimed value, not proven outcome.
Decision status. Executive, theory and strategic position are all marked Under evaluation in the decision board. This report is consistent with that policy and does not select among them.

Thor Skov Jørgensen — Adm. direktør (CEO)

Group level

What this actor needs to be prepared for

A group-level decision on whether to reduce, sell, strengthen or differentiate Coop App / Lobyco, framed as an investment trade-off across the group. The CEO must own the full executive discussion and connect digital strategy to financial pressure and bricks-and-mortar core.

T1 · Portfolio discipline and opportunity cost

Strategic question: How should Coop's CEO allocate constrained group capital between Coop App / Lobyco and physical-store priorities?
Possible main claim
App / Lobyco investment should be governed as one item in a constrained group portfolio, with explicit KPIs tying investment continuation to demonstrable store-level value.
Required situation analysis
Must establish (a) financial pressure, (b) opportunity cost across chains, (c) the gap between app reach and proven financial contribution, and (d) why marketing-derived claims cannot substitute for profitability evidence.
Supporting evidence
  • Coop 2025 results indicate revenue ~32.5bn DKK, EBITDA ~313m DKK, EBIT loss ~215m DKK (E016).
  • Case states Coop is financially pressured (E003) with declining revenue and structural deficits (E006).
  • App adoption / engagement does not by itself prove financial contribution (E013).
Weakening / nuancing evidence
  • Lobyco claims of ~25% household reach (S1) suggest the app is a non-trivial customer asset that pure cost cuts could erode.
  • Playable (S2) and Shortcut (S3) suggest existing digital investment has produced real campaign and feature capability that may have option value.
  • DI e-commerce data (E026) suggests digital channels are mainstream consumer behaviour.
Theory perspectives
Digital Business Model perspective + selected DVC concepts.
Theory parts actually used
Digital Business Model: value capture, key resources, governance/KPIs — to test whether app/Lobyco can be governed as a value logic, not only as a customer-facing experience.
DVC: digital outputs and relevance only — to test whether app reach translates into store-anchored value.
Theory concept → Coop observation → analytical meaning → strategic implication: "value capture" → app engagement is not yet linked to traceable margin → reach without capture is fragile under financial pressure → CEO needs investment guardrails before further commitment.
Supplementary sources
Used S1 Lobyco reach as claimed reach, not proven economic value.
Used S4 DVC parts to frame "value creation" vs. "value capture".
Not used S2 Playable too narrow for group-level allocation logic.
Not used S3 Shortcut is feature-focused; less useful for capital allocation.
Missing S5 Hedman & Bjørn-Andersen could support an IT-debt argument but full reference is unverified.
Source-critical issues
Avoid using Lobyco reach as profit evidence. The 232m DKK exam-context loss vs. 215m DKK official EBIT loss must be reconciled before quoting.
Possible recommendation types
Capital allocation framework; KPI gates for app investment; quarterly portfolio review; opportunity-cost statement vs. store-CAPEX.
Exam risk
May read as generic "discipline" advice if not tied tightly to the app/Lobyco question. Risk of underusing course's digital concepts.
Must investigate before choosing
Whether Coop has any internal KPI evidence linking app behaviour to basket/visit/margin; whether group-level allocation data is available; reference details for S5.

How Coop should govern customer-facing digital, loyalty, retail media and chain differentiation. This actor sits closest to Coop App / Lobyco day-to-day and must reconcile cross-chain governance with the executive-discussion options.

D1 · Hybrid governance — keep, but tie investment to KPIs

Strategic question: Can Martin set a hybrid governance model that keeps Coop App / Lobyco but conditions further investment on store-level KPIs and chain-fit?
Possible main claim
Hybrid governance — keep app/Lobyco, but gate investment on store-level KPIs, chain-fit and source-critical use of vendor claims — is consistent with both financial pressure and digital opportunity.
Required situation analysis
Must establish (a) financial pressure, (b) the gap between marketing-derived claims and proven value, (c) the case's "reduce / strengthen / go deep / other" option space.
Supporting evidence
  • Coop 2025 EBIT loss (E016).
  • Cross-competitor fit pattern (E015).
  • Source-critical inferences on engagement claims (E013, S1, S2).
Weakening / nuancing evidence
  • Hybrid framing risks sounding like compromise language unless the gates are concrete.
  • Lobyco governance complexity is not yet documented in evidence register.
Theory perspectives
DVC Framework + Digital Business Model.
Theory parts actually used
DVC: experiences, relationships, relevance.
Digital Business Model: value capture, governance/KPIs, key resources.
"Value capture under uncertainty" → app/Lobyco today → analytical meaning: continued investment requires conditional commitments → strategic implication: explicit KPI gates and chain-fit thresholds.
Supplementary sources

Used S4 DVC concepts as analytical filter.
Not used S2 Playable narrower than governance question.
Not used S3 Shortcut development history not load-bearing.
Not used S5 Historical IT-debt frame possible but not central.

"Hybrid" must not become a non-decision; gates must be specific enough to be tested.
Possible recommendation types
KPI gates for app investment; chain-fit thresholds; quarterly governance review; source-critical reporting standards for vendor claims.
Exam risk
Reads as "balanced" boilerplate if gates are vague; risk of over-broad scope drifting into CEO territory.

What KPIs the course suggests; whether internal Coop data exists for store-level KPI gates.

D2 · Lobyco as controlled strategic capability

Strategic question: Can Lobyco be retained but with a clarified mandate — Coop first, external second?
Possible main claim
Lobyco may be most valuable as a controlled capability whose primary mandate is to strengthen Coop's chains; external/platform growth should be authorised only when it strengthens Coop's own strategic position.
Required situation analysis
Must establish (a) Lobyco's role today, (b) the divestment alternative (T2) as a comparison, (c) governance risks of distraction, (d) whether external value is empirically credible.
Supporting evidence
  • Case explicitly raises Lobyco future as a discussion point.
  • Lobyco self-presentation (S1).
  • Cross-competitor fit pattern (E015).
Weakening / nuancing evidence
  • No evidence in register about Lobyco's external customer base or revenue.
  • Constraining a subsidiary may reduce its commercial momentum.
Theory perspectives
Digital Business Model + selected Platform/ecosystem concepts.
Theory parts actually used
Digital Business Model: key resources, governance, value capture.
Platform/ecosystem: multi-sided dynamics only — not full network-effect theory.
"Multi-sided dynamics with primary side" → Coop chains as primary, external buyers as secondary → analytical meaning: mandate ordering matters → strategic implication: Coop-first mandate with authorised exceptions.
Supplementary sources
Used S1 Lobyco's self-presentation as the position to govern.
Used S2 Playable retail-media frame to scope the platform side.
Not used S3 Shortcut development history not central.
Not used S4 DVC used selectively only.
Not used S5 Historical IT-debt frame possibly relevant but not load-bearing.
Source-critical issues
S1 cannot establish external commercial value of Lobyco. Avoid presenting external positioning as proven traction.
Possible recommendation types
Mandate statement; authorisation rules for non-Coop work; reporting line; protected investment in Coop-first capabilities.
Exam risk
Risk of overstating Coop as a platform; mandate language must remain governance-focused, not aspirational.
Must investigate before choosing
Public information on Lobyco's external clients; the course's treatment of platform-edge governance.

D3 · Retail media and customer relationship as the core argument

Strategic question: Should Martin frame Coop App / Lobyco primarily as a retail-media and customer-relationship infrastructure that supports Coop's business model?
Possible main claim
Coop App / Lobyco's strategic significance can be reframed as retail-media and customer-relationship infrastructure — provided that vendor claims are used cautiously and supplier-funded value is tested against store reality.
Required situation analysis
Must establish (a) retail media as a value layer, (b) supplier interactions and customer data, (c) caution on marketing-led evidence, (d) consistency with bricks-and-mortar core.
Supporting evidence
  • Lobyco retail-media narrative (S1).
  • Playable supplier-campaign mechanics (S2).
  • Cross-competitor pattern (E015).
Weakening / nuancing evidence
  • Both core sources are commercially interested.
  • No retail-media revenue figure in evidence register.
  • Risk of overstating Coop as a platform.
Theory perspectives
DVC Framework + Platform/ecosystem (selected parts).
Theory parts actually used
DVC: relationships, digital outputs, relevance.
Platform/ecosystem: supplier-customer interaction, data-mediated relationships only.
"Data-mediated relationships" → app users + suppliers via Lobyco → analytical meaning: app becomes a connective layer rather than only a customer experience → strategic implication: govern as relationship infrastructure with cautious commercial framing.
Supplementary sources
Used S1 Lobyco retail-media positioning.
Used S2 Playable supplier-campaign mechanics.
Not used S3 Shortcut development history not load-bearing.
Not used S4 DVC used selectively.
Not used S5 Historical IT-debt frame not core.
Source-critical issues
Both load-bearing supplementary sources are biased; the angle leans heavily on cautious framing. Without external retail-media data, claims must remain "indicative".
Possible recommendation types
Retail-media value-measurement model; supplier-funded campaign guardrails; chain-specific advertiser segmentation; transparency standard for marketing claims.
Exam risk
Highest source-criticism exposure of all Martin angles; depends on disciplined language to avoid overclaiming.
Must investigate before choosing
Any external retail-media benchmarks; whether the course material covers retail media specifically.

Strongest angles so far, without locking choice

Based on the current evidence register, source criticism and theory-to-evidence matrix, four angles look most defensible against red-team challenge — but no choice is made:

  • D1 · Hybrid governance (Martin) — best balance between financial pressure, digital potential and source criticism; consistent with current strategic_position_options.md scoring of "hybrid governance" at 10.
  • R3 · Chain-specific modules (Rikke) and M1 · Discount-specific app logic (Michael) — both directly answer the case's "go deep by chain" question, and both are supported by competitor-fit evidence (E015, E018, E019, E021).
  • T1 · Portfolio discipline (Thor) — most defensible CEO-level frame because it acknowledges financial pressure (E016) without dismissing app reach (S1).
  • D2 · Lobyco as controlled strategic capability (Martin) — most useful "third path" between strengthen and divest, but limited by missing evidence on Lobyco external value.

Each of these angles can still fail under red-team pressure if specific evidence gaps below are not closed.

Open decisions

  • Executive choice remains open. The strongest cross-actor candidates remain Martin (D1, D2) for cross-chain digital governance and Thor (T1, T3) for group-level investment trade-off. Michael (M1) is the strongest chain-specific candidate.
  • Strategic stance remains open. The angle work above sharpens the comparison between hybrid governance, go-deep-by-chain, controlled-capability and selective simplification.
  • Theory choice remains open. DVC + Digital Business Model continues to look strongest across the angle map; DVC + Porter/positioning becomes stronger if the paper goes chain-differentiation; DVC + Platform/ecosystem becomes stronger if retail media (D3, B3) becomes central.
  • Competitor focus remains open. Lidl and REMA are central if M1 is chosen; Salling is central if R3 is chosen; Dagrofa/MENY is central if B1 is chosen.
  • Language and citation style remain open. APA 7 is recommended but not locked.

Missing evidence

  • Internal Coop KPI evidence linking app behaviour to store-level outcomes (basket, visit, fresh-category, margin). Without this, every "value capture" claim must be cautious.
  • Lobyco external commercial evidence (clients, revenue, partnerships). Without this, T2 (divestment) and D2 (controlled capability) rely on case framing rather than independent fact.
  • Reconciliation between the case's 232m DKK loss figure and Coop's official 215m DKK EBIT loss (E007/E008 vs. E016).
  • Reference details for S1 (Lobyco), S2 (Playable), S3 (Shortcut) — URL, title, access date.
  • Bibliographic data for S4 (DVC Framework) and S5 (Hedman & Bjørn-Andersen 2016).
  • Course-material specifics on which DVC and Digital Business Model concepts are emphasised — to ensure "selected parts" choices match the course frame.
  • Retail-media benchmarks outside Lobyco/Playable marketing material — needed before D3 or B3 can be load-bearing angles.
  • Price-perception data for 365discount specifically — needed before M3 can be defended.
  • Architecture / cost evidence on chain-module feasibility in the existing app — needed before R3 and M1 can become operational recommendations.
