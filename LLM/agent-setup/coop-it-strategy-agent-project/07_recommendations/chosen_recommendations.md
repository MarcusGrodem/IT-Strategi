## Recommendation 1: Redefine App Success Metrics From Engagement To Business-Model Fit

Original option: 2

**Recommendation:**  
Martin Hasgard Olesen should argue that Coop must redefine app success metrics. The app should not primarily be evaluated by engagement, user numbers, campaign activity or gamification metrics, but by whether it supports Coop's store-based business model.

**Why:**  
The strategic question is not whether Coop App is active or visible, but whether it creates relevant value for customers, stores, chains, suppliers and Coop's financial situation.

**Evidence:**  
Lobyco, Playable and Shortcut claims can indicate app reach and intended value, but they are commercially interested sources. Coop's financial pressure and physical-store core mean output metrics must be linked to business outcomes.

**Theory link:**  
DVC distinguishes digital outputs from deeper digital value. Digital Business Model perspective asks whether digital activity supports value creation and value capture.

**Risk:**  
Coop may lack the internal KPI data needed to measure incrementality, store-level effects and profitability.

**Implementation implication:**  
Define a KPI hierarchy that separates:

- adoption,
- engagement,
- loyalty,
- store traffic,
- basket value,
- supplier-funded contribution,
- customer trust,
- profitability.

## Recommendation 2: Develop Chain-Specific App Value Propositions Within One Governed Platform

Original option: 3

**Recommendation:**  
Coop should develop chain-specific app value propositions, but within one governed platform rather than through uncontrolled separate app strategies.

**Why:**  
Coop's chains serve different customer missions. 365discount, Brugsen, SuperBrugsen and Kvickly should not necessarily be served by the same app logic.

**Evidence:**  
Competitor evidence suggests digital tools create value when they fit the business model: Lidl Plus reinforces savings, REMA uses simple store utility, Salling integrates Scan&Go with chain apps, and Dagrofa/MENY links loyalty to food/local identity.

**Theory link:**  
DVC relevance supports chain-specific digital value. Digital Business Model perspective supports differentiated value propositions while maintaining shared infrastructure and governance.

**Risk:**  
Chain-specific app value can increase complexity and cost if not governed carefully.

**Implementation implication:**  
Use one shared platform, but create chain-specific modules, messaging, offers and success metrics.

## Recommendation 3: Reduce Or Redesign Weak Engagement/Gamification Features Unless They Show Store-Level Value

Original option: 7

**Recommendation:**  
Coop should reduce, redesign or deprioritise engagement and gamification features that cannot be linked to customer relevance, store-level value or credible supplier-funded contribution.

**Why:**  
Engagement is not automatically strategic value. Games and campaigns may create activity, but the final paper should avoid assuming that activity equals profitability.

**Evidence:**  
Playable and similar supplier claims are useful as evidence of intended value, but they are marketing-biased. Claims about basket uplift, campaign engagement or app frequency need verification before they can support strong conclusions.

**Theory link:**  
DVC can assess experiences and digital outputs, but Digital Business Model perspective asks whether those outputs create value capture.

**Risk:**  
Reducing engagement features may reduce app activity in the short term.

**Implementation implication:**  
Create a feature review model:

- keep features that improve shopping, savings, relevance or store value,
- redesign features that only create shallow engagement,
- remove features that add complexity without documented value.

## Recommendation 4: Use Coop App To Strengthen Member Relationship, But Distinguish Loyalty From Profitability

Original option: 9

**Recommendation:**  
Coop should continue using the app to strengthen the member relationship, but Martin should insist that loyalty claims are separated from profitability claims.

**Why:**  
Coop's membership model is a distinctive asset, but strong member interaction does not automatically prove incremental profit.

**Evidence:**  
Coop has a large member base and app/member logic, but app-user value claims may reflect selection bias: already loyal customers may be more likely to use the app.

**Theory link:**  
DVC relationships can explain how app-based interaction may create customer value. Digital Business Model perspective tests whether that relationship creates value capture for Coop.

**Risk:**  
The analysis may overstate loyalty if it lacks data on incremental behaviour.

**Implementation implication:**  
Measure member value through incremental behaviour, retention, store visits, basket effects and customer trust, not only app activity.

## Recommendation 5: Run Chain-Level App Pilots Before Scaling Major New App Investments

Original option: 11

**Recommendation:**  
Coop should test chain-level app initiatives through pilots before scaling major app investments across the group.

**Why:**  
Hybrid governance requires evidence before scaling. Chain-level pilots can test whether different app logics actually support different customer missions.

**Evidence:**  
The source base contains uncertainty about app profitability, retail media effects and gamification value. Competitor evidence suggests fit matters, but Coop-specific chain evidence still needs strengthening.

**Theory link:**  
DVC relevance supports testing customer-specific digital value. Digital Business Model perspective supports testing value proposition and value capture before scaling.

**Risk:**  
Pilots may slow down broad app development and require internal coordination.

**Implementation implication:**  
Pilot app modules for selected chains, define success metrics before launch, and scale only if pilots show customer relevance and business-model contribution.
