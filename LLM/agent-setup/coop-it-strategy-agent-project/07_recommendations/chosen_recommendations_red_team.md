# Red Team Review — Chosen Recommendations

Date: 2026-05-04

Chosen direction:

- Actor: Martin Hasgard Olesen
- Theory: DVC Framework + Digital Business Model perspective
- Strategic stance: Hybrid governance
- Chosen recommendations: 2, 3, 7, 9 and 11

Purpose:

This review challenges the chosen recommendations before they are used in the final report.

## Executive Summary

The chosen recommendations are coherent with the locked direction. They form a cautious, source-critical and governance-oriented argument:

> Coop should not simply invest more in the app, cut the app or sell Lobyco. Martin Hasgard Olesen should argue for a governed, evidence-based app strategy where app value is measured by business-model fit, chain relevance, member value and tested store-level effects.

The set is strong because it avoids uncritical app optimism and fits both DVC and Digital Business Model thinking.

Main weakness:

The selected set may understate Lobyco and retail media, even though both are central in the case and evidence base. These should be treated as supporting issues inside the recommendations, especially Recommendation 1 and 2.

## Key Risks

### 1. Lobyco Is Not A Standalone Recommendation

Problem:

The case explicitly asks whether Coop should possibly afhænde/sell Lobyco. The chosen recommendations do not include a standalone Lobyco recommendation.

Risk:

An examiner may ask why Lobyco is not directly addressed.

Fix:

Treat Lobyco explicitly inside Recommendation 1:

> Lobyco should be governed as a Coop-first digital capability. A sale should not be recommended unless evidence shows that Lobyco's external value or cost structure outweighs its contribution to Coop's store-based business model.

Also mention in final analysis:

- The paper considered divestment.
- The evidence is not strong enough to recommend sale.
- Hybrid governance is more defensible than either uncritical retention or sale.

### 2. Retail Media Is Not A Standalone Recommendation

Problem:

Retail media and supplier-funded campaigns appear in the evidence, but they are not one of the selected recommendations.

Risk:

The final paper may underuse Digital Business Model value-capture logic.

Fix:

Include retail media under Recommendation 1 or 3:

- As a possible value-capture mechanism.
- With trust, transparency and KPI guardrails.
- Not as proven net profit.

Safe wording:

> Retail media should be treated as a possible value-capture mechanism, but not as proof that the app is financially successful.

### 3. Recommendations 2 And 5 May Overlap

Problem:

Recommendation 2 says create chain-specific value propositions. Recommendation 5 says run chain-level pilots.

Risk:

They may look like the same recommendation repeated.

Fix:

Differentiate them clearly:

- Recommendation 2 = strategic design principle.
- Recommendation 5 = implementation and risk-reduction method.

Possible final wording:

> First define chain-specific app logics; then test them through pilots before scaling.

### 4. Recommendation 3 Could Overclaim Against Gamification

Problem:

We do not have strong evidence that gamification fails. We only know that Playable is a supplier/marketing source and that engagement does not prove profitability.

Risk:

If written too strongly, the paper may make an unsupported negative claim.

Fix:

Do not write:

> Coop should cut gamification because it does not work.

Write:

> Coop should review and redesign gamification features unless they can be linked to customer relevance, store behaviour or supplier-funded value after costs.

### 5. Recommendation 4 Needs Stronger Member Evidence

Problem:

The member relationship is important, but the final report must avoid assuming that membership/app loyalty equals profitability.

Risk:

The recommendation may become generic.

Fix:

Tie it to:

- Coop's member-owned identity,
- personal offers,
- trust,
- local relevance,
- incremental behaviour,
- Digital Business Model value capture.

## Recommended Revision To Final Recommendation Set

Keep the five selected recommendations, but slightly sharpen the titles:

1. **Redefine Coop App success around business-model fit and store-level value.**
2. **Develop chain-specific app value propositions within one governed Coop platform.**
3. **Review and redesign gamification/engagement features unless they show customer or store value.**
4. **Use Coop App to strengthen member relationships while separating loyalty from profitability.**
5. **Pilot chain-level app initiatives before scaling major new investments.**

Add Lobyco as a cross-cutting governance point under Recommendation 1:

> Lobyco should be treated as a Coop-first digital capability whose mandate and investment level must be justified through the same KPI logic.

Add retail media as a value-capture point under Recommendation 1 or 3:

> Retail media should be developed only with trust and supplier-value guardrails.

## Final Red-Team Verdict

The chosen recommendation set is usable and coherent, but it needs two safeguards before final writing:

1. Lobyco must be addressed explicitly as a governance/capability issue, even if not a standalone recommendation.
2. Retail media must be addressed as possible value capture, but with trust and evidence caveats.

With those safeguards, the set fits:

- Martin Hasgard Olesen,
- DVC + Digital Business Model,
- hybrid governance,
- the official case question,
- source-critical exam expectations.
