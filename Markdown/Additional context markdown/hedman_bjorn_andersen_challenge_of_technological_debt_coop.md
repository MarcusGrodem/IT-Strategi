## Page 6

![Rendered page 6](assets/hedman_bjorn_andersen_challenge_of_technological_debt_coop_page_06.jpg)


### Visual / content notes

Visual page: section **Coop and the competitive landscape of supermarkets in Denmark**. Key facts visible:  
- Coop traces its roots to the 19th century and is Denmark's second-largest grocery chain in the case context.  
- Coop had around **1.5 million owners/members**.  
- In 2015 Coop was market leader in a competitive cut-throat supermarket market.  
- Competitor Dansk Supermarked had grown rapidly and was Coop's biggest competitor.  
- Coop operated across chains such as **Kvickly, SuperBrugsen, Dagli'Brugsen, Fakta, Irma**, plus online shops **Coop.dk** and **Irma.dk**.  
- Coop issued about **1,500,000 customer loyalty cards**.  
- The section states Coop was an old-fashioned, professional, heavy organisation, but strategically wanted to move from price competition toward convenience and value-added customer propositions.  
- Page includes **Figure 1**, a chain/store-count diagram for the Coop formats: Kvickly, SuperBrugsen, Dagli'Brugsen and Irma are visible, grouped under large supermarkets, supermarkets and local grocery concepts.

## Page 10

![Rendered page 10](assets/hedman_bjorn_andersen_challenge_of_technological_debt_coop_page_10.jpg)


### Visual / content notes

Visual page: **The new IT strategy** and **Moving forward with SAP**. Highlighted text states:  
- In early **2013**, Munk-Nissen developed a new IT strategy for Coop called the **615 strategy**.  
- The strategy was named from the three strategic metrics characterising the new IT strategy:  
  1. deliver **six times** the business value per dollar spent on IT,  
  2. deliver a **100% stable** IT operation,  
  3. by **2015**.  
- The ambition to deliver six times value required a substantial explanation. The page gives examples around infrastructure spending in Denmark versus the UK and the need to reduce cost from around **DKK 1.00** to **DKK 0.25** per unit, with an example stating Coop IT was supporting more business value with lower IT spend.  
- In **Moving forward with SAP**, highlighted text states: one of the first major chunks of technological debt to be addressed was the **HR and Finance** function; in **2012**, the board decided to integrate support functions of chains/supermarket chains to achieve economies of scale; this led to the focus on SAP.  
- Another highlighted line states the reason to start with HR and Finance was that it was a prerequisite for Coop IT to function as one integrated retail company.

## Page 11

![Rendered page 11](assets/hedman_bjorn_andersen_challenge_of_technological_debt_coop_page_11.jpg)


### Visual / content notes

Visual page: conclusion, questions and appendix map. Key points visible:  
- SAP HR/Finance introduction had stressful experiences, but management now had the impression it had introduced the most comprehensive SAP retail business module covering the full supply chain.  
- Highlighted conclusion: with the old system, Coop might have needed about **DKK 600 million** to renew infrastructure, **DKK 200 million** to renew cashier terminals, **DKK 200 million** to rationalise many servers/infrastructure, and another three-digit million investment to renew the core retail supply chain management.  
- Highlighted conclusion: The CIO had ensured Coop's biggest competitor, Dansk Supermarked, modernised first and completed SAP implementation while Coop was still running legacy but stable systems. This bought Coop time and information.  
- The page states Coop was still running many different legacy applications and asks how long the strategy of delayed modernisation could remain viable.  
- **Questions** listed:  
  1. How important is IT in the retail business? What would happen if central systems were not available for hours or days?  
  2. How do you assess the so-called high-performance IT organization put forward by Munk-Nissen? What do you see as strengths and weaknesses?  
  3. How do you assess the 615 strategy proposed by Munk-Nissen?  
  4. How do you assess the severity of the technological debt of Coop?  
  5. What would you suggest that Coop should do?  
- Visual: Appendix 1 map/diagram of Denmark showing logistics locations/warehouse network, including coloured markers for fresh goods warehouse, dry goods warehouse, specialist warehouse and non-food warehouse.
